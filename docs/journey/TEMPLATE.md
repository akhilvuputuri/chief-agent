## User-visible problem and preceding iteration

What happened, expected behavior and impact, using a sanitized example. Link the earlier entry or implementation this builds on. Explain what its evidence established and which new observation changed the next step. Do not imply sole causation from chronology alone.

## Evidence

Label each claim as reported, tested, measured, hypothesis or deferred. Link source records, commits/PRs, tests and design notes. For measurements, include date/window, collection method, sample size/denominator, units, model/configuration, workload, pricing if relevant and missing data. Distinguish live production observations, user-reported acceptance and synthetic reproduction. Store only sanitized aggregates here; link safe inspection procedures rather than private trace contents.

## Diagnosis and alternatives

Separate observed facts from possible explanations. Explain the chosen mechanism, alternatives, tradeoffs and the evidence that would change the conclusion.

## Implementation and review

Link relevant code, tests and architecture notes. Explain what changed and how it addresses the mechanism. Preserve failed checks and independent review findings with their date or reviewed revision, the correction and the final exact-head verdict; approval of an older revision is historical evidence.

## Verification and outcome

What passed or failed, with scope and limitations? What was measured versus merely implemented or tested? Use equivalent workloads for comparisons. A passing health check does not demonstrate conversational quality; a token or storage reduction does not establish financial savings. State remaining live acceptance checks explicitly.

If unfinished, state the exact pending step instead of writing a release claim. Update the status above and the journal index once evidence closes it; keep earlier review failures and candidate-stage observations dated.
